## Short description

A spending journal you explore by sight and sound: inspect uncertain purchases with AI suggestions and evidence, while keeping control of categories and limits.

## Inspiration

A table is useful when you know which transaction you want. We wanted to explore a different way to begin a short spending review: opening a book, hearing a change in status, and choosing which record deserves a closer look.

The design question is whether sound and a familiar journal form can make that review easier to enter without obscuring the facts. This is a product hypothesis; we have not established a measured improvement or completed a representative user study. A direct record index remains available for quick lookup.

## What it does

The Listening Ledger presents one ledger through two books. Daily Journal follows calendar dates; Spending Collection groups the same records by category. A page unfolds into a path of interactive nodes. Short tones communicate three states: within applicable limits, needs attention, and needs review. Opening a node reveals the amount, reason and source, with optional device reading.

Users set their own total and category limits and preview changes before saving. For an unclear purchase, they can describe its purpose and request a DeepSeek category suggestion with an exact quotation. Accepting a category is a human decision; recognizing a merchant remains a separate action.

The journal separates reading scope from the budget period. Incomplete transaction coverage cannot justify a green budget conclusion or a definite remaining allowance. A blue unresolved record can still show a confirmed overage as an additional fact.

## How we built it

The local application uses native JavaScript modules, Node HTTP, SQLite, StPageFlip, Web Audio and browser device speech. Two visual layouts share the same records and rule functions. Five AI illustrations were generated in advance for the five example spending days, then matched locally to confirmed categories and supported merchant themes. Other dates reuse appropriate themes; uncertain or unsupported content is left unillustrated. There is no image-model call on page turns. Paper and cover materials retain separately credited physical sources. Live text, amounts, paths and controls remain interactive HTML/SVG. The asset ledger distinguishes AI images from the 18 retained CC0 physical-source assets; generated images are not labelled CC0, and external photographs are not presented as our own work. The illustrations represent themes rather than actual purchased items or locations; the published image prompts contain no private financial records.

PayPal Sandbox Transaction Search is the intended source for authorized test-account purchase records. The adapter preserves event direction and status, filters supported completed purchases, tracks query coverage, and commits complete snapshots atomically. It retains prior valid data when a query fails or cannot cover the limit period. Account and currency baselines are separated. Totals are completed purchases **before refunds**, not account balances or net spending.

DeepSeek handles purpose interpretation and clarification. The server validates categories and exact input quotations before a user can adopt a suggestion. For recaps, the model is limited to selecting existing fact IDs. Program templates render the numbers and conclusions, and mandatory coverage/overspending facts cannot be omitted. AI does not set prices, alter transaction amounts, rate merchants or execute payments.

AI coding tools assisted implementation, testing and documentation. The author confirmed in headphone feedback that the revised three timbres were distinguishable and the final volume appropriate; this is one-person feedback, not a user study. State tones are generated by Web Audio; spoken details come from the available browser/OS voice, not from DeepSeek TTS. The source includes tests for amounts, coverage, stale results, provider changes and single-channel playback logic.

## Challenges

The hardest data problem was keeping a narrow reading or query window from appearing to prove that a whole budget period was within limits. We separated reading scope, budget period, queried windows and cutoff, then introduced revisioned baselines and explicit missing-data states.

We also replaced free-form AI financial summaries with verified fact selection. This allows the model to choose a reading order while keeping monetary assertions under deterministic program control.

The configured Sandbox app now supports Transaction Search. On 11 November, the existing USD 12.00 purchase appeared after reporting lag and completed the real browser workflow: live DeepSeek suggestion, separate category adoption and recognition, repeat synchronization, then service restart and browser reload. Original amount, budget and decision history were preserved; no new payment or refund was made during that verification. Synthetic examples remain separately labelled.

## What we learned

A richer interface still needs plain answers: where a record came from, what is known, what is uncertain, and who made a decision. Sound can invite attention, but color and tone must not imply that a merchant is safe or that a purchase is morally good. The record index, text explanations and stop controls are as important as the book animation.

We learned to distinguish an API capability in documentation from permission available to our actual app. Authentication, a successful empty query and an imported eligible purchase are three different results.
